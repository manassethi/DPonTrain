    How the simulator works

    On each day d before departure, the expected number of bookings is

    λ = D₀ × level × curve[d] × (fare ÷ ₹1,100)−ε

    and actual bookings are a Poisson draw from λ, capped at the seats left.

    • Realcurve[d], the share of 3A passengers who book d days out (journeys Mar–Jul 2025).
    • RealEach departure's demand level, and the calendar forecast that existed before booking opened.
    • AssumedD₀ and ε. Fares never changed in the data, so these are scenarios to test.

    The pricer solves V(d, s) = max over fares of E[fare × bookings + V(d − 1, s − bookings)] backwards from departure. That gives the fare matrix above: the best fare for every combination of days and seats left.

    EMSR-b is the airline fare-class method. The six fares are nested booking classes. For each cut-off it pools the classes above, and protects seats for them while P(their demand ≥ seats protected) ≥ the lower fare ÷ their average fare (Littlewood's rule). Textbook EMSR-b values each class at its fare and assumes passengers stay in their class. The fare-adjusted version values each class by the extra revenue opening it brings, (fare × Q − fare above × Q above) ÷ (Q − Q above), so it accounts for passengers buying down. Both re-solve the protection levels every day here.

      How the network model works

      One berth is sold several times on one run: Firozpur → New Delhi overnight, then New Delhi → Bhopal, then Bhusaval → Mumbai. The 52 stops are grouped into 9 key stations, which gives 8 legs and 36 journeys (origin–destination pairs). A booking needs a free seat on every leg it crosses.

      • RealEach journey's share of 3A passengers, and its booking curve.
      • RealBase fares from the published 3A distance fare table, plus reservation fee and superfast charge.
      • Assumedε and the demand scale, as in the single-coach model. Fares can move from 0.85× to 1.30× of base.

      The three ways to control seats

      Bid prices. A network LP over the rest of the window gives each leg a shadow price; a dynamic program per leg then turns it into a seat value that moves with every booking. A journey's seat cost is the sum of the bid prices of the legs it uses; it is offered the fare that maximises expected bookings × (fare − seat cost), and closed if no fare covers it.

      Per-journey optimizer. The single-coach dynamic program, run on each journey alone, using the seats left on its path. It ignores the other journeys competing for those seats.

      Fixed seat quotas. The coach is split up front in proportion to each journey's demand on its busiest leg, and each journey is priced within its quota.
